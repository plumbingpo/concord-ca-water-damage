Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. When it happens in Concord, Concord Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else

Sewage Cleanup, Step by Step

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle sewage cleanup from start to finish:

1

Safety Assessment

We start by measuring how far the sewage contamination reaches, noting any safety hazards and setting up containment so clean areas are not cross-contaminated.

2

Extracting Sewage and Removing Materials

Standing sewage is pumped out completely. Carpet and its padding, insulation, drywall and other contaminated porous items are removed, then disposed of properly as biohazard waste.

3

Decontamination & Sanitization

All remaining surfaces are thoroughly cleaned and disinfected using EPA-registered antimicrobial solutions. Meanwhile, air scrubbers fitted with HEPA filters pull pathogens and other contaminants from the air.

4

Dry-Out and Deodorizing

Commercial drying equipment removes all residual moisture. An industrial deodorizing treatment gets rid of the sewage smell. The space is confirmed safe to occupy by clearance testing.

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Do not wait on a slow drain, which can turn into a full blockage
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.
